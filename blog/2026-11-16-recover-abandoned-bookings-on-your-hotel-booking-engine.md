## The Bookings You Almost Had

Every day, guests visit your website, check availability, choose a room and then leave without booking. They have already found you, liked what they saw and started the process. Losing them at that point is expensive, because you have often paid to bring them there through search, metasearch or social advertising.

Abandonment is normal in online buying. The Baymard Institute, which averages 50 studies of ecommerce checkout behaviour, puts the [average online cart abandonment rate at just over 70%](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate). That figure covers online retail in general rather than hotels specifically, but the lesson applies: most people who start a purchase online do not complete it on that visit. Recovering even a small share of abandoned bookings on your hotel booking engine lowers your cost of acquisition, because the demand is already paid for.

## Why Guests Leave the Booking Engine

Not every abandoned booking is a lost guest. Some are simply researching, comparing dates or waiting to check with a travel companion. Others leave because something in the process gave them a reason to stop.

### Unexpected costs at the end

Taxes, fees or charges that only appear at the final step are a common reason to abandon. Show the full price as early as possible so there are no surprises.

### A slow or complicated process

Too many steps, long forms and pages that load slowly on mobile all test the guest's patience. Every field you ask for should earn its place.

### Doubt about the price

A guest who suspects the same room is cheaper elsewhere will often leave to check. If they find it there, the booking goes to another channel.

### Not ready to decide

Many guests are early in their planning. They want to come back later, but without a reminder they may never return to your website.

## Find Where Guests Drop Off

Before fixing anything, measure it. Set up your analytics to track each step of the booking engine, from the search for dates through to confirmation, so you can see where the biggest losses happen.

| Drop off point | What it often signals | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Search results | Price, availability or a confusing layout | Rate presentation, room photos, clear policies |
| Room and rate selection | Too many choices or unclear differences | Number of rates shown, how inclusions are described |
| Guest details form | Friction or too many fields | Required fields, mobile usability, autofill |
| Payment step | Unexpected costs or a lack of trust | Total price shown early, payment options, security cues |

A sharp drop at one step points to a specific problem. A gradual loss across every step usually means the process as a whole is too long.

## Reduce Friction Before Recovering Anything

The cheapest abandoned booking to recover is the one that never happens. Shorten the path to booking, keep the total price visible, offer the payment methods your guests expect and test the whole process on a phone, where many of your visitors arrive. We covered the mobile side in detail in [why guests drop off your hotel website on mobile](/blog/why-guests-drop-off-your-hotel-website-on-mobile).

### Show why booking direct is better

Remind guests at each step what they gain by booking with you: the best available rate, flexible conditions, a direct line to the hotel or a small extra on arrival. Clear benefits answer the doubt that sends guests to compare elsewhere.

## Bring Undecided Guests Back

Once friction is reduced, focus on the guests who genuinely were not ready to book.

### Remarketing campaigns

Visitors who searched for dates but did not book can be shown relevant ads as they continue browsing, reminding them of your hotel and the dates they looked at. A specialist team, such as [Bookassist Digital Media](https://bookassist.com/digital-media), can run these campaigns alongside your search and metasearch activity, so the budget goes to people already close to booking.

### Follow up emails, where permitted

If a guest has entered their email address and given consent under your privacy policy, a short, helpful reminder can bring them back to finish the booking. Keep it useful, mention the dates they searched and avoid pressure.

### Make it easy to return

Let guests save or share a quote, and keep their search details when they come back. Starting again from scratch is one more reason to give up.
