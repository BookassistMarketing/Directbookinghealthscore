## The Quiet Season That Sets Next Year's Revenue

While most hotels are thinking about the festive period, corporate travel managers are doing something far more consequential for your 2027 revenue. From autumn into December, companies run their annual hotel RFP process: they review last year's spend, invite properties to bid, negotiate rates and publish a preferred hotel list for the year ahead.

A place on that list is valuable. It puts your hotel in front of every traveller at that company, often inside the booking tool they are required to use. Missing the cycle usually means waiting twelve months for the next chance, while a competitor down the road collects the midweek business that would have filled your quietest nights.

## Why Hotels Miss Out on Corporate Programmes

Most independent hotels do not lose corporate business because their product is wrong. They lose it because they are not visible or ready at the moment decisions are made.

### Not bookable where business travellers book

Corporate travellers rarely search a hotel's own website first. They book through agency and corporate booking tools that draw hotel content and rates from the Global Distribution System. If your property is not loaded there with accurate content and bookable rates, you are simply not in the conversation, however strong your location.

### Slow or incomplete RFP responses

A typical RFP questionnaire asks for rates, inclusions, cancellation terms, meeting space, sustainability information and security details. Buyers often receive far more responses than they can review, so late or incomplete submissions are the first to be set aside.

### Pricing without evidence

Hotels that quote a corporate rate without knowing what that account actually delivered last year either leave money on the table or price themselves out. Buyers respond well to a property that can show it understands the account's volume and pattern of stays.

## What Travel Managers Look for in 2027 Bids

| Buyer priority | What a strong response shows |
|---|---|
| Value | A clear negotiated rate with defined inclusions such as breakfast or WiFi |
| Flexibility | Reasonable cancellation terms and last room availability where possible |
| Duty of care | Safety, security and accessibility information answered in full |
| Sustainability | Honest, specific data on energy, waste and certifications |
| Bookability | The rate loaded correctly and visible in the GDS on day one |

That last row is where many good bids fall down. A rate that has been agreed but is not loaded correctly produces failed bookings, frustrated travellers and a buyer who quietly moves the account elsewhere at the next review.

## A Practical Plan for the RFP Window

### Audit last year's corporate production

Start with the data you already have. Which companies booked, how many room nights, which days of the week and at what average rate. This tells you which accounts to pursue, which to renegotiate and where you have real leverage.

### Prepare a reusable response pack

Most RFPs ask similar questions. Keep a single, current document with your facilities, policies, sustainability data, security information and photography, so each response takes hours rather than days and arrives before the deadline.

### Make sure you are bookable in the channels buyers use

A professional GDS presence, such as [Bookassist GDS](https://bookassist.org/gds), puts your rates and content inside the systems travel management companies and corporate booking tools rely on. It is what turns a winning bid into bookings that actually arrive.

### Protect your direct and leisure mix

Corporate rates should fill need periods, not displace higher value business. Set sensible caps, blackout dates and minimum stay rules so negotiated volume supports your wider strategy rather than undermining it. ## Think Beyond the Preferred List

Not every company runs a formal RFP. Smaller firms, local employers and project teams often book on their own, and a clear business travel page on your website with a simple enquiry route can convert that demand directly at a lower cost than any intermediary. Treat the RFP season as the moment to review your whole business travel offer, not only the formal bids.
